Haiku helpers in Limen: when and how

Claude Haiku 5.5 is a cheap, fast model for narrow chores. Anthropic places it on "classification, routing, extraction, and subagent tasks"2 and keeps the larger models for complex agentic coding.1 In Limen, a Haiku helper collects evidence and the lead (Opus or Sol) decides. This page shows where Limen's logic should tell an agent when to use a helper, how to use it, and when not to.

The change is small. Put the rule in the prose the lead reads when it decides, add one helper role with a fixed evidence packet, and make the completion wake tell the truth when the packet arrives. Nothing routes automatically.

  • Haiku 5.5 helper
  • Lead (Opus or Sol), or a part that exists

1Where Limen decides to delegate

Every place where a lead hands work to another model, with the hook or file that carries the decision.

  1. Scout before a spawn. The coordinator asks for the seam, the tests that cover it, and the unknowns. The packet goes into the worker's handoff as a lead, not a map.
  2. Triage of a failed job or a failing test. First failure, code path, log tail. The lead decides to resume, steer, or stop.
  3. Review pre-pass. Each acceptance line mapped to a diff hunk and a check with real output. It can add questions. It never clears a line.
  4. Team scout in a group. One per team, after the team's own hypothesis. Today hook/group-peer.ts blocks it.
  5. Cabinet digest and candidate table for the group lead. Facts per team, no ranking. The lead pane has no LIMEN_GROUP_ID, so this uses no team slot.
  6. Judge pre-pass. Claims in research reports that have no fetched source. The judge still names where the reports diverge.
  7. A worker's own OMP scout. Later, and only after the pilot: it needs a per-subagent model setting and the Claude bridge in the worker's job.

The lead keeps every other step: hypotheses, synthesis, the hard change, landing, the review of record, the judge's verdict, the keeper, the picture, and all text Adam reads. Deterministic checks (ticket check, picture build, tests, limen status) need no model.

2Should this chore go to a Haiku helper?

The lead asks these questions in order. Any "no" keeps the chore with the lead. Haiku does not escalate on its own: in Anthropic's test, Haiku 5.5 called the Opus advisor on none of 198 questions.5 So the escalation checks belong to the lead, after the packet arrives.

Use a helper when

  • a lead decision waits on evidence
  • the chore is read-only with a clear end
  • the answer is checkable: path and line, a first failure, an evidence table
  • the packet removes more lead turns than its cold start costs

Do not use one when

  • one grep or a known file answers it
  • the chore is judgment or text for Adam
  • the pilot has not kept this chore, or the route is not proven
  • the caller is a group member without an approved slot
  • the failed job is itself a helper

Escalate to the lead when

  • the served model is not Haiku 5.5
  • the top finding is false when read
  • Verified: is none, absent, or false
  • the next step rests on a guessed finding or an unknown

The lead then does the chore itself. No second helper.

3Normal mode, before and after

One coordinator, its workers, and its reviews.

The saving comes from fewer lead turns, not a cheaper price. A lead's cost is mostly cache reads: one Opus job (live records panel, F934) took 164 turns and read 27.3 million cached tokens. Ten fewer exploration turns at 150K context save about 1.5 million. A helper job costs a worktree, a cold start of about 10 to 15 thousand prompt tokens, and one wake round trip. On the Max plan this is quota, not money.

The packet reaches the lead whole only if it fits the wake: 15 lines and 1,200 characters (handoffExcerpt in src/job/wake-text.ts). The clip removes the tail first, so Verified: goes on line 3, not last.

4Group mode, before and after

A lead pane, team coordinators, team workers, the cabinet, and the research judge.

Each team scouts after it publishes its own hypothesis, from its own approach note. Scouts are never shared across teams, so the teams stay independent. Team workers still cannot launch helpers.

Today hook/group-peer.ts blocks the task tool and subagent spawns for every member, and templates/coordinator.md says there is no helper-agent launch. A team scout therefore needs Adam's rule change. The lead's digest and candidate table do not: the lead pane has no LIMEN_GROUP_ID, so the block does not apply to it, and its helper is outside the team allowance.

5Where the steering lives

Each signal sits where the agent reads it at the moment of choice. Numbers give the order of change.

Prose carries the rule because the coordinator reads templates/agents.md on every call (hook/communication.ts, before_agent_start). Jobs do not get that file, so a helper's rules go in its own role prompt. Mechanism is added only for facts that prose cannot know: which model answered, how many tokens it used, and wake text that is wrong today.

A signal for the moment of decision goes in the message stream (the wake), not in a conditional system-prompt line. The prompt cache is a byte-exact prefix, so a system-prompt change invalidates everything after it.5 Contradictory rules also cost accuracy,5 so the fix is to correct the wake strings that contradict the table, not to add a second rule. Claude Code steers the same way: a per-subagent model field and a tool allowlist. Its read-only Explore agent uses the main model unless a project sets model: haiku.7

Order

  1. Add claude-haiku-5-5 to the local OMP Claude bridge. Today --model pi-claude/claude-haiku-5-5 exits 0 and runs Haiku 4.5.
  2. Add templates/scout.md: one question, no edits, no commits, the packet, and the Verified: rule.
  3. Save the requested model at spawn. One session reader shows the served model and the token totals in limen jobs.
  4. Fix the wake: a mismatch line, and "nothing to land" for a scout. In the same slice, fix the OMP wake that says "before landing" for a failed job (src/integrations/coordinator-wake.ts).
  5. Run the pilot in pilot.md. After it keeps a chore, add the table to agents.md and the helper model to the board.
  6. If the pilot keeps triage: one clause in the failed-job wake.
  7. After Adam's allowance answer and a real group trial: the team-scout line and the matching group-peer.ts rule.
  8. After the pilot proves the route: a worker's own scout through the OMP overlay.

Not proposed: a hint at spawn from the task's shape (no pre-spawn event, and a free-text classifier is noisy), a wake-side Verified: parser, a reminder in group peer messages, a hint when cabinet events pile up, any conditional system-prompt line, and any automatic routing or model fallback.

What Adam decides

  1. Approve the pilot in pilot.md, with the bridge entry as its first step.
  2. Group helpers: a team scout uses one of the team's launch slots (both teams and the study recommend this), or a separate helper allowance. Either way, the "no helper agents" lines change only by Adam's decision.
  3. Board model: after at least three pilot rows, whether the helper model joins the board's standing models.
